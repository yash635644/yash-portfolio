Lost Money to an Online Scam in India? What to Do in the First Hour

Quick answer

Call 1930, India's National Cyber Crime Helpline, as soon as you realise you have been cheated, then file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in, call your bank to block the card or UPI and dispute the transaction, and save every screenshot and transaction ID. Speed matters most: the sooner your complaint reaches the banks involved, the better the chance they can freeze the money before it moves to other accounts.

Key takeaways

  • Call 1930 first. Every minute gives the money more time to move.
  • Keep the transaction ID (UTR), amount, time and the scammer's UPI ID or number ready.
  • Report to your bank as well. Under RBI rules, reporting within three working days protects you in many cases.
  • Do not delete chats or messages. They are your evidence.
  • Anyone who promises to "recover" your money for a fee is running a second scam.

Why the first hour matters

Scammers rarely keep stolen money in one account. They move it quickly through several "mule" accounts, then withdraw it or convert it. A complaint on 1930 lets police alert the banks involved so they can put a hold on the money while it is still sitting in an account.

Police often call this the "golden hour", though estimates of how short the window really is range from 15 minutes to a couple of hours. Delays of days make recovery much harder. Recovery does happen: in one two-month period, Mumbai Police reported restoring ₹3.97 crore to 6,096 victims through 1930. Even if you are reading this days later, report it anyway.

Step 1: Call 1930 right now

1930 is the National Cyber Crime Helpline run under the Ministry of Home Affairs. Before you call, put these details in front of you:

  • The transaction ID or UTR number from your bank SMS or UPI app
  • The amount, date and exact time of each payment
  • Your bank name and the account or card used
  • The scammer's UPI ID, phone number, bank account or website, if you have them

Write down the acknowledgement or complaint number you are given. You will need it for everything that follows.

Step 2: File a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in

Go to the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and choose the financial fraud option. Add the same details and upload screenshots. If you already called 1930, mention that complaint number.

For larger losses, the government has been rolling out an e-Zero FIR: financial fraud complaints above ₹10 lakh made on 1930 or the portal are registered as a Zero FIR automatically, and you then visit a cyber police station within three days to convert it into a regular FIR.

Step 3: Call your bank and block what was used

Call your bank's official customer care number, which you can find on the back of your card or on the bank's own website (official Indian bank websites now use the .bank.in domain). Ask them to:

  • Block the debit or credit card, net banking or UPI that was used
  • Register a dispute for the unauthorised transaction and give you a reference number
  • Note the 1930 or portal complaint number against your dispute

Under the RBI's 2017 customer protection rules, if a fraud happened because of a breach elsewhere in the system (not your fault and not the bank's) and you report it to your bank within three working days, your liability is zero, and banks must credit the disputed amount back to your account within 10 working days while they investigate. If you shared your OTP, PIN or password, the rules are less protective, which is one more reason to report fast. You can read the rules in the RBI circular.

Never search for "bank customer care" on Google or social media in a hurry. Fake helpline numbers are one of the most common scams in India. Use the number on your card, passbook or the bank's official website.

Step 4: Save every piece of evidence

  • Screenshots of chats, messages, payment requests and the scammer's profile
  • Bank and UPI SMS with transaction IDs
  • Call logs with numbers and times
  • Website addresses, app names and any files you were asked to install

Do not delete the chat or block the scammer before you have screenshots, and do not argue with them or try to get the money back yourself.

Step 5: Secure your phone and accounts

  • Change your UPI PIN, net banking password and email password.
  • Uninstall any screen-sharing or remote-access app the scammer asked you to install.
  • Check which SIM cards are issued in your name on Sanchar Saathi (TAFCOP) and report any you do not recognise.
  • If money vanished without any OTP, lock your Aadhaar biometrics on myAadhaar.

Step 6: Report the number so others are warned

Report the fraud call, SMS or WhatsApp number on Chakshu on the Sanchar Saathi portal. It helps get numbers disconnected, even if you did not lose money.

Step 7: Follow up and escalate

  • Track your complaint on the portal using the acknowledgement number, and visit the cyber police station if you are asked to.
  • If your bank does not resolve your complaint within 30 days, escalate to the RBI Ombudsman. It is free.
  • Write formal letters to your bank and the cyber cell. Cyber Kavach has ready complaint letter formats in English and Hindi.

Beware of "recovery" scams

After a fraud, many victims are contacted by "agents", "lawyers" or "cyber experts" who promise to get the money back for a fee. This is almost always a second scam. Real recovery only happens through 1930, the cyber police and your bank, and none of them charge you for it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get my money back after online fraud in India?

Sometimes, yes. If you report quickly on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, police can ask banks to freeze the money while it is still in an account, and courts or banks can later release it to you. The chances drop the longer you wait, so report immediately.

What details do I need when I call 1930?

The transaction ID or UTR number, the amount, date and time of each payment, your bank and account, and any details of the scammer such as their UPI ID, phone number, bank account or website.

Does my bank have to refund money lost to fraud?

It depends on how the fraud happened. Under RBI rules from 2017, you have zero liability if the fraud was caused by a breach elsewhere in the system and you report it to your bank within three working days. If you shared your OTP or PIN, the bank may hold you responsible for losses until you report it.

What should I do if it has been several days since the fraud?

Report it anyway on 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in, inform your bank in writing, and keep your evidence. Recovery is harder after a delay, but a complaint is still needed for any investigation and for your bank dispute.
